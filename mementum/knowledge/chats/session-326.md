Sure — the core is just the chain rule applied systematically.

## Setup

A network is a composition of functions:

$$\hat{y} = f_L(f_{L-1}(\cdots f_1(x)))$$

with a loss $\mathcal{L}(\hat{y}, y)$. Each layer typically computes $z_l = W_l a_{l-1} + b_l$, then $a_l = \sigma(z_l)$. We want $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial W_l$ for every layer.

## The chain rule, factored

Naively, $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_1}$ requires multiplying Jacobians through every layer. Backprop's insight is that these products **share prefixes**, so you compute them once, back-to-front.

Define the error signal at layer $l$:

$$\delta_l \equiv \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_l}$$

**Output layer** (e.g. softmax + cross-entropy collapses beautifully):

$$\delta_L = \nabla_{a_L}\mathcal{L} \odot \sigma'(z_L) \quad\text{(= } \hat{y} - y \text{ for softmax+CE)}$$

**Recurrence** — the error flows backward through the transpose of the weights:

$$\delta_l = (W_{l+1}^\top \, \delta_{l+1}) \odot \sigma'(z_l)$$

**Gradients** — local error × local input, an outer product:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_l} = \delta_l \, a_{l-1}^\top \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b_l} = \delta_l$$

Then the update: $W_l \leftarrow W_l - \eta \, \delta_l a_{l-1}^\top$.

## Why it's efficient

- **Forward pass:** compute and cache all $a_l$, $z_l$.
- **Backward pass:** one sweep computing $\delta_L \to \delta_1$, each step a single matrix-vector product.
- Cost ≈ 2–3× a forward pass, versus exponential if you expanded the chain rule per-weight. It's dynamic programming on the chain rule — reverse-mode automatic differentiation, which is optimal when outputs (1 scalar loss) ≪ inputs (millions of weights).

## The structural read (project-relevant)

Two things worth noticing given our frames:

1. **The write channel is $\delta$, not the data.** The weight update $\delta_l a_{l-1}^\top$ is *error* × *activation* — exactly the §3 forged-exposure claim (write channel ≡ error ¬data). A weight only moves where the error signal and the input activation are simultaneously nonzero.

2. **Magnitude ∝ ∫(δ · a) dt.** Each weight integrates its per-step outer-product contribution over training — that's the literal mechanism behind "magnitude is the evidence" and the AMP-TRAJECTORY accumulation read. Sign settles when the integrand's sign becomes consistent; magnitude accumulates while it stays consistent; contested coordinates see δ·a flip sign and cancel toward net ≈ 0.

That's the whole algorithm: cache forward, propagate $\delta$ backward through $W^\top$, outer-product for gradients.

## Source (verified verbatim)

**Paper:** *"Trees to Flows and Back: Unifying Decision Trees and Diffusion
Models"* (Ramachandran & Sra 2026, arXiv:2605.00414).

**Naming correction:** the "C" in CGTSM is **Continuous**. GTSM is the
general principle; **CGTSM is its continuous-time SDE instantiation**. Our
older pages (`score-matching-compression.md`, `diffusion-holographic-
isomorphism.md`) conflate the two. The paper's headline unification is
**decision trees ↔ diffusion** (gradient boosting builds trees, so our
"gradient boosting ≡ diffusion" gloss is downstream-correct, not the title).

### Definition 3.1 (Continuous Global Trajectory Score Matching)

For an ideal SDE with law P\* and scores s\*_t(x), and a model s_θ(x,t):

```
L_CGTSM(θ) = ½ ∫₀ᵀ w(t) · E_{p*_t} ‖ s_θ(x,t) − s*_t(x) ‖²_{D(t)} dt

w(t) > 0                    strictly positive weighting function
‖v‖_D = √(vᵀ D v)           diffusion-induced (semi-)norm
D = σσᵀ                     diffusion tensor of dX_t = b(x,t)dt + σ(x,t)dw_t
                            (positive definite once rank-deficient σ disallowed)
```

### Theorem 3.2 (CGTSM Optimality Implies Path Matching)

Achieving **zero** CGTSM loss for **any** strictly positive weighting
w(t) > 0 is **necessary and sufficient** for matching the full path-space
measures, i.e. **Pθ = P\***.

**Proof sketch (Girsanov).** The KL divergence between path-space measures
D_KL(P\* ‖ Pθ) is an integral of the squared difference between the process
drifts. The reverse-time drift is a function of the score, so that difference
reduces to the CGTSM integrand. The loss is zero iff the KL divergence is
zero. (Full proof: paper §F, Corollary F.3.)

## The mechanism — how it narrows the search space

Girsanov turns the codimension intuition ("each layer adds a constraint")
into an exact statement:

```
D_KL(P* ‖ Pθ) = ½ ∫₀ᵀ E‖ b*(x,t) − bθ(x,t) ‖²_D dt     KL between path measures
              = ½ ∫₀ᵀ E‖ s*_t(x) − sθ(x,t) ‖²_D dt       reverse drift = fn(score)
```

The CGTSM integrand **is** the KL integrand. Three consequences:

### 1. Marginal vs joint — the actual reason the space shrinks

- **Endpoint loss** (cross-entropy / next-token) matches only the
  **terminal marginal** P\*₀ — a single time-slice of the trajectory.
  Infinitely many path measures share one marginal.
- **GTSM** forces D_KL between the **full path-space measures** to zero
  ⟹ **Pθ = P\***. The feasible set collapses from *"all processes with the
  right terminal slice"* to *"the single process P\*."*

You pin the whole **joint law over trajectories**, not one slice of it.
This is the search-space narrowing stated exactly.

### 2. Why "any w(t) > 0" works → density matters, weighting does not

A non-negative integral ∫ w(t) g(t) dt with w > 0 and
g(t) = E‖score diff‖²_D ≥ 0 is zero **iff g(t) = 0 almost everywhere**,
independent of the shape of w. Weighting cannot *hide* a nonzero-error
region — it can only **fail to cover** one. If w(t) = 0 on a set, that
interval is unconstrained → a hole in the narrowing.

This is the formal backing for the state.md line
**"density of measurement matters, weighting does not"** — but with a
**critical scope correction** (s205, full-paper read): this holds **only for
the zero-loss fixed point**. For **finite-budget optimization** the weighting
*does* matter. Prop F.6: λ(t) injects a deliberate bias to **counter-balance a
learner's intrinsic coarse-first tendency**, forcing attention onto harder
fine-grained detail. Since we always train at finite budget, **our α=5.0 cosine
weighting is exactly such a bias choice — load-bearing, not arbitrary.**

### 3. The correct metric is set by the noise geometry

Corrections are measured in **‖v‖_D = √(vᵀDv), D = σσᵀ** — the local
diffusion tensor, not flat L2. Depth-dependent standing-wave amplitude

invariant proxy for ‖·‖_D**. Confirmed as the literal object, not a guess.

## Why endpoint losses are ill-posed (the degeneracy GTSM removes)

The endpoint-feasible set contains a large **degenerate manifold**: configs
where one layer's error E_l is cancelled downstream by −E_l ("compensating
errors"). These look correct on the terminal marginal but diverge internally
and fail out-of-distribution. GTSM lifts that floor — each step must match
*independently*, so cancellation is not representable. An **ill-posed inverse
problem becomes well-posed.**

## One objective, two solvers — diffusion AND gradient boosting

The paper's headline (s205 full read): GTSM is a **single master objective**
(integrated score error over the whole trajectory) solved by **two algorithm
families**. The GTSM integral is **decomposable into per-chunk consistency
checks** — that decomposability is what lets disparate algorithms solve one
problem.

| | How it solves GTSM | Paper anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion / score nets** | minimize the **entire integral at once** (one parametric score net) | Def 3.1, Thm 3.2 |
| **Gradient boosting** | **greedily** reduce the largest remaining error, chunk by chunk (add weak learners) | Def 3.3, Thm 3.4 |

**⚠ "GD" = functional gradient descent = gradient boosting over weak learners
(decision trees) — NOT parameter-space SGD on a neural net.** The duality is
"diffusion score-net ↔ gradient-boosted tree ensemble," both as GTSM solvers.

### The bridge (three load-bearing results)

1. **Residual = score (Thm E.22).** For squared-error loss the boosting
   residual `y − F_m(x)` is proportional to the optimal meta-score
   `∇_F log p(y|F_m(x))`. **Fitting residuals (boosting) IS denoising score
   matching (diffusion).** The keystone.
2. **Greedy = globally optimal (Thm 3.4 / E.30).** The discrete GTSM objective
   is **additively separable** with deterministic transitions ⟹ by **Bellman's
   principle** the myopic per-step choice is the global optimum. Boosting is the
   *optimal* solver, not a heuristic.
3. **Girsanov closes the diffusion side (F.3).** Reverse-drift difference =
   score difference ⟹ path-space KL = ∫ squared score error ⟹ zero loss ⟺
   Pθ = P\*.

### Standard diffusion losses are CGTSM special cases

- **Simple/unweighted loss** (Ho et al. 2020) = CGTSM with w(t)=1 (Prop F.5).
- **Weighted loss** λ(t) (Karras, Kingma) = CGTSM with w(t)=λ(t) (Prop F.6).
- **Consistency distillation** = bootstrapped CGTSM approximation that biases
  *coupling between adjacent timesteps* for fast sampling (Prop F.7).

### Two limits the paper states plainly

- **Idealized / asymptotic.** "Gradient boosting (in an idealized version) is
  *asymptotically* optimal." Exact equivalence (Cor E.25) needs **infinite data,
  realizability, convergence**; Thm 3.4 needs **continuous limit + rich-enough
  weak learners**.
- **Rests on Tree-to-Flow dyadic refinement (Thm 2.5).** Trees-as-jumps would
  break it; refinement smooths splits into continuous paths so the **Novikov
  condition** for Girsanov holds (Remark F.4). Irreducible jumps ⟹ Lévy
  machinery, out of scope.

## The precondition most people miss

GTSM narrows the space **only when a target trajectory exists**:
- **distillation / our compression** — the teacher *is* the trajectory;
- **diffusion** — the forward noising process *manufactures* a trajectory
  with an analytically known score target.

Standard LLM **pretraining is endpoint-only** (data gives the next token,
not the intermediate residual stream) — so GTSM's leverage does **not**
apply directly there. Diffusion's deep trick is to *invent* a path to match,
converting an underdetermined endpoint problem into an overdetermined
trajectory one. CGTSM says trees and diffusion are doing the same move.

## Caveat (epistemic — keep before any published claim)

Theorem 3.2's exactness is for **SDEs with a shared diffusion tensor σ**.
"Depth = time, residual update = score" is an **idealization** of that SDE;
a discrete-depth transformer is not literally an SDE with known σσᵀ.

- The **narrowing principle transfers** (measured: compensating-error
  degeneracy removed, 27%→37%).
- The **literal Pθ = P\* guarantee does NOT transfer for free** — it is
  contingent on the SDE idealization and on cosine being a faithful enough
  stand-in for ‖·‖_D. **IOU**: this contingency is unverified for our setting.

- **But it's contrastive, not regression.** TSP's loss is DPO-style ranking;

# Trajectory-compile — make the wire legible and portable

> s305, Michael: "we have the GTSM loss function, and you just found a depth-timing
> measurement. If you look at the SuperBake paper in refs/ it may inform a design."

## The convergence (three independent lines on one design)

1. **s305 depth-timing (measured, ours).** The country materializes on the one-shot
   landmark prompt only at L24 (the s305 decodability cliff), while the native h-hop
   has already consumed its input by then (capital_leak 0.62 at L24 on a clean
   country prompt). The two hops **overlap in depth** → no static write at L24 can
   route (five constructions inert, s303–s305).

2. **SuperBake (`refs/superbake.txt`, Ruehlman 2026) — the law from the other side.**
   *"a single-layer linear map fights with only the layers above it, while SGD's early
   deposits ride nineteen native nonlinear layers of amplification; the network is the
   kernel, and it is upstream."* Their first constructed linear solve plateaued at
   **58%**; the fix was writing composition **enrichment at 0.16× depth** (≈L6), where
   native subject→attribute machinery lives. Their transport law — *"read a payload at
   write-layer +1; it does not survive many blocks; quiet directions attenuate ~30×"* —
   is our `reinject_landed 0.033` verbatim. Our late writes violated their laws.
   **But** SuperBake composes *known* facts (inject the answer-entity early, per-fact =
   a lookup); our wire needs the model's own *inferred* country → construction hits the
   same depth wall (their §8 boundary: keys "go key-dead at depth").

3. **GTSM (`gtsm-search-space.md`, Thm 3.2).** Endpoint losses match only the terminal
   marginal → admit **compensating-error** solutions (a layer's error cancelled
   downstream): correct at the output, wrong internally. gd_cd (s303) used endpoint KL
   and **its G4 pin-mechanism was UNMET** — almost certainly an answer-shortcut, not a
   materialized intermediate. GTSM's dense per-depth match removes exactly that
   degeneracy; Prop F.6 says at finite budget, spike the weighting where it matters —
   **SuperBake supplies where (the enrichment + readout bands).**

**The design.** Take the one thing that WIRED (gd_cd gradient) and (a) widen its LoRA
band to reach the enrichment band so gradient can reshape the EARLY layers, (b) replace
endpoint KL with a **GTSM depth-dense trajectory loss** to the teacher's own CoT,
SuperBake-weighted. Prediction: the country now materializes **early and legibly** (G4
closes), the wire generalizes (F1–F3), and it ternarizes to a portable plate (s304).
This is the s299 auto-superbake lifecycle made precise: **construction laws shape the
trajectory targets; gradient (the GTSM search) finds the legible, portable delta.**

### Thinking

Michael's asking about the loss function and GTSM loss for the lay-a-negative micro experiment. The key distinction is that endpoint loss only constrains the terminal marginal distribution, leaving a huge solution space, while GTSM applies dense score matching across the entire trajectory to pin down the full path measure through Girsanov theory — though this requires an actual target trajectory to work with, either from a teacher or synthetically generated.

For the micro model trained on lambda calculus, the kernel reduction trace itself becomes the ground-truth trajectory: the reduction steps and their depth directly define the target path. This means we can replace the endpoint CE loss with a trajectory-based loss that matches the actual reduction sequence.

The deeper insight is how this complements lay-a-negative: commit-freeze modifies the write side (optimizer), while GTSM modifies the read side (loss signal). With endpoint CE loss, the gradient δ at each coordinate is ambiguous since many trajectories fit — leading to contested coordinates and sign oscillation. With GTSM's dense per-layer error signal, each layer gets its own clear feedback, so δ becomes better-conditioned and signs commit earlier and more coherently, making the negative lay much cleaner. The two modifications work together: GTSM narrows the search space so decisions become unambiguous.

The prediction is that GTSM loss enables faster sign-commitment with fewer contested coordinates, so commit-freeze costs less function. Under CE-only loss, early sign-commitment is risky because the gradient sign depends on downstream errors that shift during training — compensating error configurations can flip signs later. GTSM breaks that coupling by fixing each layer's target to the kernel trajectory, making the integrand's sign stationary and trustworthy for commitment.

Now I'm considering what trajectory GTSM needs for micro-from-scratch training. The options are using kernel reduction as a target (though that requires aligning reduction steps to depth), manufacturing one internally, or distilling from an already-trained standard model and re-recording the trajectory.

The distillation approach is interesting because it solves the precondition problem while implementing the re-record path — the student starts fresh but learns from a positive-laid teacher. However, without a teacher and using only cross-entropy loss, the non-stationarity problem returns: δ becomes non-stationary and commits happen too early. So the loss function choice (cross-entropy versus GTSM-dense) directly determines safety, and I should test the interaction between loss type and optimizer (standard versus commit-freeze) as a 2×2 matrix.

The trajectory loss approach appears to work cleanly based on the s306 results, and the data-derived weighting for GTSM is mentioned in the consensus-opcode-loss documentation. For the micro model specifically, uniform weighting works fine since it only has 4 layers, and using a standard-trained sibling as the teacher gives a stationary target. The depth versus reduction-time question remains open though — I shouldn't conflate those two concerns.

## The link: the loss function *defines* the integrand

Go back to the backprop recurrence:

$$\delta_l = (W_{l+1}^\top\, \delta_{l+1}) \odot \sigma'(z_l)$$

Under an **endpoint loss** (CE-only), every layer's error signal is manufactured by backprop through the *downstream weights* — which are themselves moving. So the integrand $\delta \cdot a$ at any coordinate is **non-stationary**: its sign depends on compensating-error configurations elsewhere in the net that will shift as training proceeds. GTSM's degeneracy argument, restated at the coordinate level: the endpoint-feasible set is huge, so a coordinate's gradient sign is only meaningful *relative to the current position on the degenerate manifold*. A sign that looks committed at step 500 can honestly reverse at step 5000 because the downstream context it was compensating against moved.

Under a **GTSM dense trajectory loss**, each layer gets a direct error term against a **fixed target** (teacher trajectory / kernel trace):

$$\delta_l^{GTSM} = \underbrace{w(l)\,\nabla_{h_l}\,d(h_l, h_l^*)}_{\text{stationary — } h_l^* \text{ is frozen}} + \underbrace{(W_{l+1}^\top \delta_{l+1}) \odot \sigma'}_{\text{shrinking as downstream matches}}$$

The dominant component of the integrand points at a target that *never moves*. Sign-consistency becomes a property of the coordinate's relationship to $h_l^*$, not of the current compensating-error configuration.

## Why this decides the lay-a-negative design

The commit-freeze fixer trusts early sign-commitment. That trust is exactly what the loss choice governs:

- **CE-only + fixer:** the Schmitt trigger will latch signs that are transiently consistent on the degenerate manifold → **premature fixing** of coordinates whose true sign wasn't decided yet. The negative gets fogged by the loss's own ill-posedness — and if function degrades, we can't tell whether "magnitude was load-bearing" or "the endpoint loss made the sign decisions untrustworthy." Confounded verdict.
- **GTSM + fixer:** decisions are made against a stationary per-layer target → commitment is honest → freezing at commitment should be nearly free *if* sign-is-the-decision is right. Clean verdict.

Which means the natural design isn't two arms, it's a **2×2**: loss ∈ {CE-endpoint, GTSM-dense} × optimizer ∈ {standard, commit-freeze}. The interaction term is the pre-registerable prediction: **commit-freeze costs little under GTSM and real function under CE-only.** If that interaction shows, we've demonstrated something bigger than either idea alone — *the search-space narrowing is what makes sign decisions trustworthy enough to fix*. GTSM decides where the decision is well-posed; the fixer makes it permanent. One narrows, the other collapses.

## The precondition, and how micro satisfies it

The page's key caveat: GTSM only has leverage **when a target trajectory exists** — pretraining is endpoint-only because data gives you the next token, not the residual stream. For micro we have two honest ways in:

1. **Re-record (the §3 path, literally):** train a standard micro first (CE-only, the positive) → freeze it as teacher → train a virgin sibling with GTSM trajectory loss + commit-freeze against the teacher's per-layer states. This *is* "RE-RECORD, don't train" — distilling an accumulated positive into a deliberately-laid negative. Your laid-positive concern stays satisfied: the **student substrate** is virgin; the positive only serves as the trajectory oracle. And s306 already gave us an advisory datum in this direction: the trajectory-loss wire ternarized **losslessly** (retention 1.0, second independent datum) — trajectory-matched deltas already lean toward the crystal.
2. **Kernel trace as trajectory (the deeper, riskier option):** micro is trained on lambda calculus and we *own* the kernel — ground-truth intermediate reduction states exist. But mapping reduction-time onto depth is an imposed register hypothesis (and s317 says reduction is tape-resident in LLMs, not depth-resident) — so this is a separate research question, not a free lunch. Option 1 first.

Two disciplines to carry in from the page's own scars: **w(l) placement must be measured, not narrated** (audit #11 — though at 4 layers, uniform-with-measured-spikes is nearly moot), and the SDE→transformer idealization caveat rides along (narrowing transfers; literal Pθ=P* does not — the s198/s210 measurements are the license, not Thm 3.2).
